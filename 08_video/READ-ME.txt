VIDEO

Three phone clips, 1080p, 163 MB between them. They are here for one thing the
stills cannot give: continuity. A photograph tells you what is on a wall; these
tell you what is next to what, and in which order you meet it walking in.

  01_pan-360-sweep_t1704.mp4          31 s   the room from the doorway
  02_pan-360-sweep_t1705.mp4          30 s   the same sweep, a minute later
  03_pan-floor-and-low-pipework...    35 s   floor level, the low runs and gully

WHAT THEY WERE USED FOR
  The wall strip across the top of the survey diagram follows the order of 01.
  That is the whole reason it reads as "stand in the doorway, turn right, and
  this is what you pass" rather than as an arbitrary arrangement of boxes.
  03 covers the floor, which every standing shot cuts off at the ankle.

DO NOT EXTRACT FRAMES FROM THESE
  They are 1080p, so 2.1 megapixels. The photosphere in 04_plant-room/01 is
  39 megapixels of the same room from a fixed optical centre. Any still pulled
  out of these clips is a downgrade on a photograph that already exists, and a
  worse one still on the reprojections in 09_panoramas/, which were made from
  that sphere and can be measured off. Use video for sequence, stills for fact.

SIZE, AND WHY THEY ARE STILL HERE
  All three sit past GitHub's 50 MB per-file warning line, which C16 reports
  every run as a NOTE rather than a failure. That is deliberate and the note is
  meant to stay visible:

      the hard limit is 100 MB per file and the largest here is 57 MB
      the site limit is 1 GB and the whole repository is well under half of it
      the survey embeds no video, so nobody in the plant room downloads these

  So they cost nothing to the thing that has to work in a basement with no
  signal, and they are irreplaceable in the ordinary sense: the room can be
  photographed again, but not as it stood on the day it was surveyed. If the
  repository ever does approach the site limit, these are the first candidates
  to move, and the honest way to move them is somewhere they stay watchable,
  not to re-encode them down to nothing.

TIME TOKENS ON VIDEO
  MP4 records a creation time in UTC that marks the END of the recording. The
  token here is the START, local time, which is what you would have looked at
  on a clock. tools/media.py subtracts the duration and shifts the zone, so
  MEDIA.tsv and the file name agree and C23 can check them against each other.
