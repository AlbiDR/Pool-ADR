THE POOL ITSELF

Two photographs, taken 52 seconds apart from opposite ends on 2026-09-09 at
13:42. Until they arrived this archive documented the plant room, the balance
room, the drawings, the manuals and the chemicals, and held not one picture of
the thing all of it exists to serve.

  01   from the far end, looking down the length toward the steps. The house
       is on the right, the clipped hedge and the deck on the left.
  02   from the steps end, looking back. The same pool with the sides
       reversed: house left, hedge and deck right.

WHAT THEY ARE GOOD FOR
  The shape and the layout, which no other file here shows. A long rectangle
  of dark water with a timber deck on all four sides, a tall hedge down one
  long side and the house down the other, its terrace looking straight onto
  the water. The steps are at the short end 02 was taken from.

  The overflow, best in 02. The water runs right up to the deck on the hedge
  side with no coping and no visible lip, and passes underneath it. That is the
  infinity edge, on the long side opposite the white wall, and it is the whole
  reason there is a balance tank next door. In 01 you are looking along the
  same edge from the other end.

  And they show why this pool cannot be judged by looking at it. Both frames
  read as a black mirror: at a shallow angle a dark finish returns reflected
  sky, not water, and in 02 the clouds are sharper on the surface than anything
  under it. Cloudiness here has to be read straight down against a submerged
  white target, never along the surface. The survey's water section gives that
  method and the scale that goes with it.

WHAT THIS FOLDER IS NOT FOR
  MEASURING ANYTHING. Both were shot on the phone's ultra-wide, and this is not
  a guess: the EXIF says 2.15 mm at f/2.2, an 18 mm equivalent, which is the
  0.6x lens. At that focal length the near deck is stretched, the far end is
  compressed, and parallel lines converge hard. Length, width and depth all
  read wrong, and reading them off anyway is a mistake this archive has already
  made once: a volume estimate taken from these frames came out roughly a third
  low and was withdrawn.

  The pool's volume is 70 to 75 m3 from the installation documentation and
  family knowledge. It has never been measured, and when it is, it will be with
  a tape in daylight and not with a camera. FACTS.txt holds the figure and
  every dose in the survey hangs off it.

WHAT IS MISSING
  A clarity reference frame: the water photographed straight down against the
  white floor grate, so one day can be compared with the next on the survey's
  own scale. One was taken on 2026-09-09 and deliberately not kept. Shooting a
  replacement is a two-minute job and worth doing on a clear day, as the
  baseline every later reading is compared against.

  A dimensioned plan of the pool, which would close the volume question and
  with it the salt charge, the flocculant dose and every other figure quoted
  per cubic metre.
