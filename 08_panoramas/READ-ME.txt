SAFE: REPROJECTIONS  (05 to 08)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  05_wall-control-and-filter_rectilinear_3000x2250.jpg
  06_wall-pipework-and-gully_rectilinear_3000x2250.jpg
  07_wall-manifold-four-drops_rectilinear_3000x2250.jpg
  08_corner-level-column_rectilinear_3000x2250.jpg

These are flat, straightened views reprojected from ONE source image:
04_plant-room/01_photosphere-216deg_t1626.jpg, a 39-megapixel equirectangular
photosphere (full sphere 15492 x 7746, so 43 pixels per degree; the crop covers
azimuth 70.9 to 286.8 and elevation +24.6 to -72.9).

Why they can be trusted. A photosphere is shot from a single optical centre, so
there is no parallax to resolve. The reprojection is gnomonic: every output
pixel is one bilinear sample of one source location. There is no feature
matching, no homography estimation, no seam finding and no blending anywhere in
the process, so there is nothing with which to invent geometry. Straight lines
in the room come out straight, which is the whole point.

Parameters, so any of these can be reproduced exactly:

    file  azimuth  elevation  h-fov   outside sphere coverage
    05      125      -22       80        0.0 %
    06      190      -25       75        0.0 %
    07      228      -42       72        0.3 %
    08      259      -30       50        2.8 %

  Elevation is positive up. "Outside coverage" is the share of the frame that
  falls beyond the photosphere's edge and renders black; it is reported because
  a large number there means the view was aimed off the sphere.

VERIFIED: photograph 07 was checked by counting. Four drops, four red handles,
four pipes entering the floor. It also shows the floor gully to the LEFT of the
drops, which is where the owner said it was. That count is the standing test
for any wide image of that wall.

NOT SAFE: STITCHES  (01 to 04)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  01_control-wall_6863x2511.jpg
  02_room-arc_3052x1385.jpg
  03_electrical-panel_stitched_5569x3951.jpg
  04_electrical-panel_single-frame_4080x3072.jpg   <- this one is a single frame

  01, 02 and 03 were stitched from multiple hand-held frames. DO NOT COUNT
  ANYTHING ON THEM and do not measure from them.

  Why, plainly: an earlier stitch of the manifold wall drew SIX drops where
  there are FOUR, and it did not look wrong. The room is 2.55 by 1.96 m, so you
  cannot stand more than about a metre and a half from any wall, and the pipes
  sit at several different depths. A single homography can only be correct for
  one plane, so near multi-depth subjects duplicate. A two-frame stitch of that
  same wall still showed five. This is a property of the capture geometry, not
  of the effort spent, and the fix is to reproject a photosphere instead, which
  is what 05 to 08 are.

  They are kept rather than deleted because they are part of the record of how
  this survey was built, and because 04 is a genuine single frame and therefore
  fine. But 05 to 08 supersede 01 and 02 for every practical purpose.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF YOU WANT MORE WIDE VIEWS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Shoot another photosphere, from a tripod or with your elbows locked, rotating
  about one point. Do not walk along a wall. A rotation has no parallax and
  reprojects cleanly; a translation does not and never will.

  The 07_video pans are 1080p, so frames from them would be a downgrade on the
  39-megapixel sphere and are not worth extracting.
