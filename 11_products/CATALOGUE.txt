NAME THEM BY NUMBER. NEVER BY "THE GRANULAR ONE".
===============================================================================
  THREE of the items below carry the word GRANULARE on the label:

      1  CORRETTORE PH PIU' GRANULARE      raises pH
      3  CLORO GRANULARE 56% RAPIDO        chlorine
      4  CORRETTORE PH MENO GRANULARE      lowers pH

  Items 1 and 4 are pH correctors pointing in OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS, and all
  three are white granules in tubs. "The granular" identifies nothing, and
  at the shelf, in a hurry, it is the shape of an accident rather than a
  vagueness: reach for the wrong tub between 1 and 4 and you have moved the
  pool the wrong way and then have to move it back, and confusing either
  with 3 puts a chlorine donor where an acid was meant or the reverse. Item
  3 carries EUH031: contact with acids liberates chlorine gas.

  AND EVERY NUMBER CARRIES ITS UNIT. 1.5 means two different things here and
  both are thresholds: 1.5 BAR on gauge 4 is the backwash trigger, where the
  dial turns green to red, and 1.5 MG/L is the top of the free chlorine band
  on the comparator card. One says wash the filter, the other says the water
  is correctly sanitised. A bare "watch for 1.5" points at both.

  SO EVERY INSTRUCTION, WRITTEN OR SPOKEN, NAMES THE NUMBER AND THE LABEL.
  Not "200 g of the granular" but "200 g of item 3, CLORO GRANULARE 56%
  RAPIDO". The number is unambiguous and the label is what is printed on
  the thing in your hand; either alone can be misread, and together they
  cannot. This rule exists because the looser form was used on 2026-09-12
  and was rightly challenged.

IF SOMETHING IS WRONG WITH THE WATER
===============================================================================
Doses are for 72.5 m3. Test first, dose second, re-test before dosing again.

CLEAR BUT SLIGHTLY CLOUDY, or dull
  Fine particles passing the sand. This pool's weak point.
  -> FLOCCULANTE 1,088 g pre-diluted into the balance tank. Backwash every
     4-5 hours of filtration, repeat until clear.
  -> Then keep it away: 72.5 g each evening after every backwash.
  -> First check the filter gauge, AGAINST THIS INSTALLATION'S BASELINE OF
     1.18 BAR, not against an absolute number. That figure was measured on
     2026-09-09 on clean freshly backwashed sand, and the gauge's own face is
     banded green up to 1.48. Kripsol's manual gives 0.5-0.7
     and a 1.0 trigger, but that describes the filter alone, not this filter
     behind an oversized pump on limiting pipework with the cell in circuit.
     Near 1.5 the filter is the cause and no chemical will fix it. Sitting at
     1.2 it is NOT the filter, and reaching for sand would be chasing a fault
     that is not there.

CLOUDY AND THE WATER IS HARD, or you can see scale
  Calcium coming out of solution.
  -> SVERNANTE 725-1,450 g. But only once the water is already clear.

GREEN, OR SLIPPERY WALLS
  Algae.
  -> ANTIALGHE 1.45 L initial, then 363 mL weekly.
  -> Expect foam. Expect the cell to work harder while the quat is present.

CHLORINE READS LOW THOUGH THE CELL IS RUNNING
  Either the cell is not producing, or the chlorine is locked up.
  -> Check the TRi PRO first: No Flow, Check Salt, Output Fault, ACL Low.
     Check salt is above 4 g/L. Check the cell is not scaled.
  -> If the cell is fine, measure CYANURIC ACID. Above 50 mg/L is lock-up.
     Target is 25-30 mg/L outdoors.
  -> The cure is dilution: drain part and refill. RECUPERO is a workaround,
     and do NOT shock with the granular chlorine while doing it.

pH KEEPS CLIMBING
  Normal for a salt pool: electrolysis drives pH up. The acid drum should be
  handling it.
  -> Check the acid dosing works before touching anything: is the drum empty,
     is the peristaltic tube perished, has the pH probe been calibrated in
     the last two months, does the display read "pH ---"?
  -> Only then, by hand: pH MINUS 290-435 g per 0.1 pH unit.

pH TOO LOW
  Usually over-correction.
  -> pH PLUS. Lapi tub: 290-435 g per 0.1 pH. NortemBio tub: 1,450 g per 0.1
     pH. THEY ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE BY VOLUME.

CELL NOT PRODUCING, "CHECK SALT"
  -> Salt below 4 g/L. One 25 kg sack raises this pool 0.345 g/L.

SHUTTING DOWN FOR WINTER
  -> SVERNANTE 3.6 L, pre-diluted, pumps running 4-8 hours, then stop the
     plant. Water must be clear first.

WHAT IS MISSING FROM THE SHELF
===============================================================================
  This is the shelf's own view: what a bottle-by-bottle audit finds absent.
  The survey's "What to buy" section is the same gaps ordered by what they
  are blocking, with the words to type into a search box. If the two ever
  disagree, the survey is the one written to be acted on.
  A CYANURIC ACID TEST. Three products on this shelf depend on knowing that
  number and none of them can tell you it. Without it, item 6 is guesswork.
  THIS IS NOW THE TOP GAP. Item 11 arrived on 2026-09-10 and answers pH and
  free chlorine, which leaves cyanuric acid as the one number that matters
  here and cannot be measured. It only ever goes up: every dose of the
  granular chlorine is sodium dichloroisocyanurate and brings its own
  stabiliser with it, roughly 1.2 mg/L a time on this pool. At 50 mg/L the
  chlorine locks up, and the only cure is dilution.

  FRESH pH 7,5 BUFFER SOLUTION, soluzione tampone pH 7,5. Still missing:
  item 12 is 7.01 and 10.01, and neither can calibrate this unit. Promoted to the
  top of this list on 2026-09-12, when the TRi PRO's pH probe was found
  reading 7.0 against a tablet test of 7.8 to 8.2. Nothing else will fix
  that: a probe can only be corrected against a liquid whose true pH is
  already known, and this is that liquid. It came in the unit's box years
  ago as item 9, dyed bluish to distinguish it from the red probe's
  ORP 700 mV buffer. Buffer keeps a year or two sealed and much less once
  opened, because it absorbs CO2 and drifts. AN EXPIRED BOTTLE IS WORSE
  THAN NONE: it calibrates the probe confidently to the wrong number.
  Buy single-use sachets, which cannot have drifted unopened. A few euros.
  While ordering, an ORP 700 mV sachet for the red probe too: that one
  falls due the moment the cell starts producing.

  A TOTAL ALKALINITY TEST. Promoted on 2026-09-10 when the TRi PRO threw
  ERRORE PH and listed "CONTR. ALCALIN. TOTALE" as its fourth suggested
  action. Alkalinity is the buffer: at a high TAC the unit injects acid
  every two hours and the pH does not move, which is indistinguishable
  from a broken probe or an empty drum. Zodiac's own balance table in
  manual 03 wants 80 to 150 ppm and a monthly reading. Nothing here can
  give either.

  A DPD No.3 TABLET, to go with item 11. No.1 alone reads free chlorine;
  the difference between No.1 and No.3 is the combined chlorine, which is
  what tells you a shock is needed rather than more of the same.

  AN UNSTABILISED CHLORINE for shock, calcium hypochlorite or similar. The
  Recupero and Svernante labels both call for one and there is none here.

  A SULPHATE TEST, given years of sulphuric acid dosing. Lower priority than
  it first appeared: the pool is finished in smooth black resin, not tile, so
  there is no grout for sulphate to attack. What the 360 mg/L limit still
  protects is the balance tank's coating and any place the pool's resin has
  failed and exposed the shell. Worth one reading, not an urgent one.

  SODA ASH IN THE PLANT ROOM for acid spills. You own 25 kg of it as item 1.
  It is simply in the wrong place.
