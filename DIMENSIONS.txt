HOW THE TWO ROOMS SIT TOGETHER

  The balance tank runs PERPENDICULAR to the plant room, not alongside it.
  It extends away from the plant room's bottom wall, aligned beneath the
  bottom-right projection and flush with the right-hand wall, and it runs
  3.00 m out beyond the plant room's footprint.

  Taken off the scaled plan: the tank's right edge is flush with the plant
  room's right edge, and its left edge sits 4 cm inboard of the projection's
  left edge. The projection is 0.95 m wide, the tank 0.91 m.

  The shared wall is therefore the underside of that bottom-right projection,
  about 0.95 m of wall. That is exactly the corner where the level column,
  the waste stack and the two low wall penetrations stand, in photographs
  04_plant-room/32 and 04_plant-room/34. Everything lines up.

  The hatch is at the FAR end of the tank, 3.00 m from the shared wall. The
  four pipe connections are on the wall at the near end. This is confirmed by
  05_balance-room/02, which looks down the length of the tank from the hatch
  with the penetration wall at the far end.

  Access consequence: to reach any of the four connections you enter through
  a small high hatch and traverse the full 3.00 m of a chamber 0.91 m wide
  and 2.00 m deep, on a ladder. Single opening, below ground, no second exit.

  Combined underground footprint: 4.52 + 2.73 = 7.25 m2.

WHAT THESE NUMBERS MEAN

  Tank size against the pool. This has to be read off the volume the tank can
  actually reach, 3.69 m3, not the coating line. Against a pool of 70 to 75 m3
  that is 5.1 percent, and the working band alone is 3.8 percent. Design
  practice for a deck level pool starts at 5 to 10 percent, so THE TANK IS
  ADEQUATE RATHER THAN GENEROUS: it sits at the floor of that band. An earlier
  version of this file said 7.5 percent and called it correctly sized; that
  used the coating line, which the tank never reaches.

  Bather surge is not the constraint. At the design figure of 80 litres per
  bather, ten people getting in at once takes 800 litres out of the tank,
  which is 29 cm of level. The tank handles a full house comfortably.

  Backwash is the constraint. Two to three minutes at something like 18 to
  26 m3/h, plus the rinse, is roughly 900 to 1,500 litres, which is 33 to
  55 cm out of a working band that is exactly 1.00 m. Ten bathers take another
  29 cm, so a backwash with a full pool of people in it would use 62 to 84 cm
  of the 100 available. DO NOT BACKWASH WITH A FULL POOL OF BATHERS. This is the arithmetic behind the note on the printed sheet that
  the balance tank level drops during a backwash and will stop the pump.
  The practical rule that follows: top the tank up before backwashing.

  Flooding. If the balance tank emptied into the plant room, its normal
  contents of 3.69 m3 over 4.52 m2 of floor would stand 0.82 m deep, and
  1.21 m in the worst case where the safety overflow had blocked and the tank
  had filled to the coating line. The pool overhead holds roughly
  six times the plant room's entire air volume. The sump pump N is the only
  drain the room has.

ALSO ESTABLISHED 2026-09-07
  There is a LOW-LEVEL PUMP INTERLOCK. The printed sheet says so in the
  backwash procedure, and it was confirmed by accident: the pump sat on M for
  a full day with an empty tank and never started. It is a backstop, not a
  protection to rely on, because the clear column that feeds it has its own
  ISOLATOR, the red-handled valve at the column's foot, on the take-off between
  the tank and the column. Shut, it cuts the column off from the tank: the
  floats then read whatever stale level the column was left holding, and the
  interlock permits a dry run. Keep it open. It is drawn on the survey diagram
  and has an entry in the legend.

  The reintegro discharges into the RETURN MANIFOLD, so mains water reaches
  the pool through the four wall outlets. It does not fill the tank; the tank
  fills from the pool spilling over its overflow edge. Reported on site
  2026-09-09: the pool is ringed by a wooden deck and the water passes beneath
  it. The overflow is an INFINITY EDGE ON ONE SIDE ONLY, the long side of
  roughly 12 m. (Stated as 12 m here; elsewhere the pool length is given as
  about 13 m. Both are estimates by eye and neither has been taped.)

  PLAN SHAPE, from a freehand sketch supplied 2026-09-09. Topology only: the
  sketch was explicitly not to scale and nothing here is measured off it.
    - A RIGHT TRAPEZOID: two 90 degree corners, so both ends run square off
      the high wall and the overflow edge is the slanted side.
    - The overflow runs the full length of that slanted side, roughly 12 m.
    - The "high wall" is opposite it and is the base.
    - Stairs occupy one end, inside the footprint, so the water surface covers
      them and they do not need adding separately.

  BECAUSE it is a right trapezoid, the surface area needs only three tape
  measurements and no trigonometry:

        area = (left end + right end) / 2  x  high wall

  The high wall is slightly shorter than the 12 m overflow edge, by an amount
  that depends on how much the two ends differ: 11.8 m if they differ by 2 m,
  11.6 m if by 3 m, 10.9 m if by 5 m.

  That gives TWO independent routes to the same number. The hydraulic
  measurement at crest gives the true wetted surface directly, including the
  stairs and any irregularity; the three tapes give it geometrically. If they
  agree, the figure is settled. If they do not, one of the assumptions behind
  the hydraulic method has failed and that is worth knowing too.

  Consequence worth keeping: a 12 m weir passing 1,300 to 3,300 L/h carries
  only 0.6 to 1.2 mm of head above the crest. The pool surface is therefore
  pinned to within about a millimetre once it spills, which is why the tank
  column can be used to measure the incoming flow directly.

THE LEVEL COLUMN, MEASURED 2026-09-10
  Heights up the column, by tape. 1: 64 cm.  2: 76.  3: 86.5.  4: 92.5.
  WHITE indicator line: 99.5.

  The spacing is uneven, 12 then 10.5 then 6 then 7 cm, so these are not a
  ruler. They are operating levels somebody marked, and two of them showed
  their meaning the same night: the pump settled at EXACTLY 3 once the circuit
  balanced, and the top-up on A stopped just under the WHITE.

  At 27.3 L/cm the whole marked span, 1 to WHITE, is 35.5 cm and 969 litres.
  Hold that number next to the backwash: 900 to 1,500 L is 33 to 55 cm. A
  single backwash is as large as everything the column can show you, or larger.
